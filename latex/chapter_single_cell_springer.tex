\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Bulk quantitative proteomics measures the ensemble average of millions of cells, masking stochastic transcriptional bursting, rare functional sub-populations, and cell-to-cell biochemical heterogeneity. The emergence of single-cell proteomics by mass spectrometry (SCP-MS) provides an unprecedented window into cellular phenotype, post-translational states, and signaling cascades at true single-cell resolution. However, transitioning from bulk microgram inputs to picogram-level single-cell samples alters the physical, statistical, and computational rules of mass spectrometry. Unlike single-cell genomics or scRNA-seq, proteins cannot be amplified enzymatically by PCR; every detected ion represents a surviving physical molecule extracted from a single cell.

Consequently, SCP datasets are dominated by ultra-low signal-to-noise ratios, severe Poisson ion-sampling shot noise, high identification sparsity ($>40$--$60\%$ missing values), and complex nested acquisition structures (multiplexed isobaric runs combining single cells with high-abundance carrier and reference channels). This chapter establishes the rigorous mathematical, biophysical, and bioinformatic foundations of single-cell proteomics. We formalize the physics of ion transmission efficiency, analyze the carrier channel paradigm and its quantitative trade-offs, and dissect the \texttt{scp} framework, which extends Bioconductor's \texttt{QFeatures} and \texttt{SingleCellExperiment} classes into a unified multi-assay data tree. We examine cell-level quality control, robust feature aggregation, leakage-free normalization, and the acute hazards of naive missing-data imputation. The chapter culminates in an executable, end-to-end R laboratory, the ``Five Deadly Sins'' of single-cell proteomics, and multi-tier exercises with exhaustive step-by-step worked solutions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Biophysical and Analytical Foundations of Single-Cell Proteomics}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{The Picogram Challenge: Why Proteomics Cannot Rely on PCR}

A single mammalian cell (e.g., a lymphocyte or epithelial cell) contains approximately $100$ to $200\;\text{pg}$ of total protein, corresponding to roughly $10^9$ to $10^{10}$ individual polypeptide molecules distributed across $10^4$ distinct protein species. In single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq), picogram amounts of mRNA are reverse-transcribed and amplified through exponential PCR cycles to generate microgram quantities of sequencing cDNA. In proteomics, no enzymatic mechanism exists to copy or amplify peptides. Every detected ion represents a physical molecule that survived sample lysis, reduction, alkylation, enzymatic digestion, liquid chromatography, ionization, and mass filtering.

\subsection{Ion Transmission Efficiency and Poisson Shot Noise}

Consider a moderately abundant intracellular protein present at $N_{\text{cell}} = 10^5$ copies in a single cell. The number of ions $k$ reaching the mass spectrometer detector is governed by the cumulative transmission efficiency $\eta_{\text{total}}$:
\begin{equation}
\eta_{\text{total}} = \eta_{\text{prep}} \cdot \eta_{\text{LC}} \cdot \eta_{\text{ESI}} \cdot \eta_{\text{optics}} \cdot \eta_{\text{MS}}
\end{equation}
In state-of-the-art ultra-low-loss microfluidic platforms (e.g., nanoPOTS~\cite{kelly2020nanopots} or cellenONE dispensing), total transmission efficiency typically ranges from $\eta_{\text{total}} \approx 0.1\%$ to $1.0\%$ ($10^{-3}$ to $10^{-2}$).

Therefore, the expected number of detected ions $\lambda$ is:
\begin{equation}
\lambda = \mathbb{E}[k] = N_{\text{cell}} \cdot \eta_{\text{total}} = 10^5 \times 0.001 = 100 \;\text{ions}
\end{equation}
Ion counting in an orbital ion trap or time-of-flight detector follows a Poisson counting process:
\begin{equation}
P(k \mid \lambda) = \frac{\lambda^k e^{-\lambda}}{k!}
\end{equation}
The variance of a Poisson random variable equals its mean ($\operatorname{Var}(k) = \lambda$). Consequently, the theoretical lower bound on technical noise, known as the Poisson shot noise limit, dictates the Coefficient of Variation ($\text{CV}$):
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:poisson_cv}
\text{CV}_{\text{Poisson}} = \frac{\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(k)}}{\mathbb{E}[k]} = \frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{\lambda} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}
\end{equation}
For an abundant protein with $\lambda = 100$ detected ions, the irreducible physical shot noise is $\text{CV} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{100}} = 10\%$. For a low-abundance regulatory protein ($N_{\text{cell}} = 1{,}000$ copies; $\lambda = 1$ ion), $\text{CV} = 100\%$. Technical measurement variance in single-cell proteomics is physically dictated by the finite quantum nature of ion sampling.

\section{Multiplexed Isobaric Architectures: The Carrier Proteome Paradigm}
% =============================================================================

To overcome the identification sensitivity barrier in data-dependent acquisition (DDA), Slavov and colleagues developed Single-Cell ProtEomics by Mass Spectrometry (SCoPE-MS and SCoPE2~\cite{slavov2021scope2}). SCoPE2 utilizes multiplexed isobaric chemical tags (TMT or TMTpro) to couple single cells to an isobaric ``carrier'' proteome.

\subsection{Design Architecture of a Single-Cell TMT Multiplex}

A standard 16-plex TMTpro single-cell acquisition incorporates three functional channel classes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Carrier (Booster) Channel (e.g., TMT-126):} Contains peptide digest equivalent to $100$ to $200$ cells. In MS1 full scans, the carrier co-elutes with identical single-cell peptides, elevating precursor intensity above the instrument trigger threshold for MS2 fragmentation.
    \item \textbf{Reference Channel (e.g., TMT-127N):} Contains a pooled aliquot (typically $5$ cell equivalents) representing all biological conditions in the experiment. The reference channel serves as an invariant internal standard for cross-batch normalization.
    \item \textbf{Single-Cell Channels (e.g., TMT-127C through 134N):} Up to 14 channels dedicated to individual isolated single cells.
    \item \textbf{Negative Control (Empty) Channel:} Contains carrier buffer and digestion enzymes without any loaded cell, providing a baseline measurement of non-specific chemical background and isotopic contamination.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The Carrier Dilution and Ion Suppression Trade-Off}

While the carrier channel enables peptide identification, excessive carrier loading introduces severe quantitative distortion. The C-trap or ion routing multipole of an Orbitrap instrument has a finite total ion capacity ($C_{\text{cap}} \approx 10^5$--$10^6$ charges) to prevent space-charge repulsion:
\begin{equation}
C_{\text{total}} = C_{\text{carrier}} + C_{\text{ref}} + \sum_{j=1}^{m} C_{\text{single},j} \le C_{\text{cap}}
\end{equation}
If the carrier channel represents a 200-fold excess over individual single cells:
\begin{equation}
\frac{C_{\text{carrier}}}{\sum_{j=1}^{14} C_{\text{single},j}} \approx \frac{200}{14 \times 1} \approx 14.3
\end{equation}
Over 90\% of the ion trap capacity is consumed exclusively by carrier ions. Single-cell reporter ions are crowded out, reducing the number of sampled single-cell ions $\lambda_{\text{single}}$ and dramatically inflating Poisson shot noise according to Eq.~(\ref{eq:poisson_cv}).

\begin{pitfallbox}
\textbf{The 200-Cell Carrier Limit:}\\
Studies by Vanderaa, Gatto, and Slavov demonstrated that carrier levels exceeding 200 cell-equivalents saturate the Orbitrap detector dynamic range, exacerbate co-isolation ratio compression, and suppress single-cell reporter ions into electronic baseline noise. The optimal carrier level is $50$ to $100$ cell-equivalents.
\end{pitfallbox}

\section{The \texttt{scp} Data Model: Hierarchical Multi-Assay Architecture}
% =============================================================================

Handling single-cell proteomics requires coordinating multiple levels of biological and chemical granularity: individual PSMs, collapsed peptides, consolidated proteins, across multiple TMT multiplexes and cell annotations.

The Bioconductor package \texttt{scp} (Vanderaa \& Gatto~\cite{vanderaa2021scp}) extends the \texttt{QFeatures} container by storing each acquisition multiplex as a dedicated \texttt{SingleCellExperiment} (SCE) assay.

\centering

\small
\textbf{QFeatures Container}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Assay 1 (SCE):} \texttt{Run01\_PSMs} ($p_1$ PSMs $\times 16$ channels)
    \item \textbf{Assay 2 (SCE):} \texttt{Run02\_PSMs} ($p_2$ PSMs $\times 16$ channels)
    \item \dots
    \item \textbf{Joined Assay (SCE):} \texttt{all\_psms} ($\sum p_i$ PSMs $\times n$ total cells)
    \item \textbf{Aggregated Assay (SCE):} \texttt{peptides} ($p_{\text{pep}}$ peptides $\times n$ total cells)
    \item \textbf{Protein Assay (SCE):} \texttt{proteins} ($p_{\text{prot}}$ protein groups $\times n$ total cells)
\end{itemize}
\end{tcolorbox}
\caption{The hierarchical assay topology of \texttt{scp}. PSMs from independent TMT runs are linked through feature annotations, aggregated into peptides and proteins, and preserved within a single reproducible container.}
\label{fig:scp_tree}

The \texttt{SingleCellExperiment} assay structure integrates:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{\texttt{assay(sce)}:} Quantitative matrix of reporter ion intensities (rows = features; columns = cells).
    \item \textbf{\texttt{colData(sce)}:} Cell-level metadata (batch run, TMT channel, cell type/condition, total intensity, cell diameter).
    \item \textbf{\texttt{rowData(sce)}:} Spectral identification metadata (peptide sequence, master protein accession, search engine score, PEP, reverse/contaminant flags).
    \item \textbf{\texttt{reducedDims(sce)}:} Dimensionality reduction embeddings (PCA, UMAP, t-SNE) computed on cell profiles.
\end{itemize}

\section{Cell Quality Control, Sparsity, and Normalization}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Cell-Level Quality Control Metrics}

Before biological analysis, single-cell channels must undergo rigorous quality filtering to eliminate technical failures:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Empty Wells / Failed Labeling:} Channels exhibiting median reporter intensity $< 3 \times$ the negative control channel or fewer than $300$ identified peptides are flagged and discarded.
    \item \textbf{Cellular Doublets:} Channels exhibiting total reporter ion intensity $> 2.5 \times$ the median single-cell intensity represent two cells captured within a single well.
    \item \textbf{Median Relative Reporter Ion Intensity (RI):} The ratio of single-cell reporter intensity to the reference channel:
    \begin{equation}
    \text{RI}_{ij} = \frac{y_{ij}}{y_{i,\text{ref}}}
    \end{equation}
    High-quality single cells should maintain a stable median relative intensity distribution across the proteome.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Leakage-Free Multi-Stage Normalization in SCP}

Normalization in multiplexed SCP must correct for two orthogonal technical axes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Intra-Assay Sample Loading Normalization:} Equalizes total protein loading across single-cell channels within a single TMT multiplex:
    \begin{equation}
    y_{ij}^{\text{SL}} = \frac{y_{ij}}{\sum_{k=1}^p y_{kj}} \times \left( \frac{1}{m} \sum_{c=1}^m \sum_{k=1}^p y_{kc} \right)
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Inter-Batch Internal Reference Scaling (IRS):} Uses the reference channel (present in all multiplexes) to remove run-to-run batch effects:
    \begin{align}
    \bar{R}_i &= \left( \prod_{b=1}^B R_{ib} \right)^{1/B} \quad (\text{geometric mean across batches}) \\
    S_{ib} &= \frac{\bar{R}_i}{R_{ib}} \\
    y_{ijb}^{\text{IRS}} &= y_{ijb}^{\text{SL}} \times S_{ib}
    \end{align}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The Sparsity Fallacy: Why Bulk Imputation Fails in Single-Cell Proteomics}

In single-cell proteomics, missing values routinely exceed 40--60\% of the data matrix. Missingness is governed by a hybrid biophysical mechanism:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Missing Not At Random (MNAR):} Low-abundance peptides whose reporter ions fall below the instrument limit of detection.
    \item \textbf{Missing Completely At Random (MCAR):} Stochastic precursor selection during DDA duty cycles, where peptide ions are present in the cell but not selected for fragmentation.
\end{itemize}

Applying standard bulk imputation algorithms (e.g., $k$-nearest neighbors or random forest) across a 50\% missing single-cell matrix creates artificial correlations, collapses cell-to-cell variance, and forces cells into spurious synthetic clusters. The gold-standard recommendation in single-cell proteomics is to perform dimensionality reduction and differential abundance directly on filtered, non-imputed matrices, or utilize model-based approaches (such as Hurdle models or mixed-effects models) that account for missingness explicitly.

\section{Hands-On R Laboratory: The Complete \texttt{scp} Workflow}
% =============================================================================

We now construct an end-to-end, reproducible R laboratory using the \texttt{scp} and \texttt{QFeatures} architecture. We simulate a multi-channel single-cell proteomics experiment (14 single cells across two biological states, 1 carrier channel, and 1 reference channel), audit cell QC, filter contaminants, normalize, aggregate features, and perform PCA visualization.

\begin{rchunk}{Simulating a 16-Plex Single-Cell TMT Multiplex}
# -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Simulation of a 16-Plex TMTpro Single-Cell Experiment
# Channels: 1 Carrier (100x), 1 Reference (5x), 1 Empty, 13 Single Cells
# Single Cells: 7 Naive Stem Cells, 6 Primed Stem Cells
# -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
set.seed(303)

n_psms <- 800
channels <- c("TMT_126_Carrier", "TMT_127N_Ref", "TMT_127C_Blank",
              paste0("TMT_Naive_", 1:7), paste0("TMT_Primed_", 1:6))
n_channels <- length(channels)

# Base log2 peptide abundance
base_abund <- rnorm(n_psms, mean = 12, sd = 2.0)

# True biology: 80 PSMs are up-regulated in Naive cells (2.5-fold, log2FC = +1.32)
bio_effect <- rep(0, n_psms)
bio_effect[1:80] <- 1.32

# Channel scaling factors (relative loading)
channel_loadings <- c(100, 5, 0.05, rep(1.0, 7), rep(1.0, 6))

raw_psm_mat <- matrix(0, nrow = n_psms, ncol = n_channels)
colnames(raw_psm_mat) <- channels
rownames(raw_psm_mat) <- paste0("PSM_", sprintf("%04d", 1:n_psms))

for (j in 1:n_channels) {
  loading <- channel_loadings[j]
  is_naive <- grepl("Naive", channels[j])
  
  # Calculate expected intensity
  expected_log2 <- base_abund + (if (is_naive) bio_effect else 0)
  expected_linear <- (2^expected_log2) * loading
  
  # Inject Poisson shot noise + additive baseline
  shot_noise <- rnorm(n_psms, mean = 1.0, sd = 1.0 / sqrt(pmax(1, expected_linear * 0.05)))
  raw_psm_mat[, j] <- pmax(10, expected_linear * shot_noise + rnorm(n_psms, mean = 50, sd = 20))
}

# Construct Channel Metadata (colData)
col_meta <- data.frame(
  Channel   = channels,
  CellType  = c("Carrier", "Reference", "Blank", rep("Naive", 7), rep("Primed", 6)),
  WellIndex = 1:16,
  row.names = channels,
  stringsAsFactors = FALSE
)

# Construct Feature Metadata (rowData)
row_meta <- data.frame(
  PSM_ID   = rownames(raw_psm_mat),
  Protein  = paste0("PROT_", sprintf("%03d", rep(1:160, each = 5))),
  Peptide  = paste0("PEP_", sprintf("%03d", rep(1:400, each = 2))),
  PEP      = runif(n_psms, 0.001, 0.08),
  Reverse  = sample(c(rep("-", 760), rep("+", 40))),
  stringsAsFactors = FALSE
)

cat(sprintf("Simulated %d PSMs mapping to 400 peptides and 160 proteins.\n", n_psms))
cat(sprintf("Carrier Intensity: %.2e | Mean Single-Cell Intensity: %.2e\n",
            median(raw_psm_mat[, 1]), median(raw_psm_mat[, 4:16])))
\end{rchunk}

\section{The Five Deadly Sins of Single-Cell Proteomics}
% =============================================================================

\begin{pitfallbox}
\textbf{Deadly Sin 1: Excessive Carrier Channel Loading ($>200\times$ Booster)}\\
\textit{Mechanism:} Spiking in a 500-cell or 1,000-cell equivalent carrier channel to maximize the total number of peptide identifications.\\
\textit{Mathematical Consequence:} Ion trap space-charge saturation. Over 98\% of trapped ions belong to the carrier, compressing single-cell reporter ion counts down to 0--10 ions per scan. Quantitative precision collapses due to severe Poisson shot noise ($\text{CV} > 40\%$).\\
\textit{Remedy:} Restrict carrier channels to $50\times$--$100\times$ single-cell equivalents.
\end{pitfallbox}

\begin{pitfallbox}
\textbf{Deadly Sin 2: Imputing High-Sparsity SCP Matrices with Bulk MCAR Algorithms}\\
\textit{Mechanism:} Applying $k$-nearest neighbors (KNN) or Random Forest imputation across an SCP matrix with $>50\%$ missing values.\\
\textit{Mathematical Consequence:} Bulk imputation assumes low-percentage missingness. In ultra-sparse matrices, KNN connects cells based on missingness patterns rather than authentic abundance, generating artificial clusters that mimic novel cell sub-types.\\
\textit{Remedy:} Filter features by minimum detection threshold ($>50\%$ across cells) and utilize model-based statistical frameworks (such as mixed-effects models) that handle missingness naturally.
\end{pitfallbox}

\begin{pitfallbox}
\textbf{Deadly Sin 3: The Pseudoreplication Fallacy (Cells as Biological Replicates)}\\
\textit{Mechanism:} Treating 50 single cells isolated from a single cell culture dish or a single patient biopsy as 50 independent biological replicates in a two-sample $t$-test.\\
\textit{Mathematical Consequence:} Severe Type I error inflation ($p$-values under-estimated by orders of magnitude). Cells within the same culture dish share identical micro-environmental, media, and passage history.\\
\textit{Remedy:} Employ hierarchical linear mixed models ($\sim \text{Condition} + (1 \mid \text{BiologicalReplicate}) + (1 \mid \text{Run})$) to account for correlation among cells from the same origin.
\end{pitfallbox}

\begin{pitfallbox}
\textbf{Deadly Sin 4: Normalizing Single Cells Without Excluding the Carrier Channel}\\
\textit{Mechanism:} Applying global column sum or median normalization to a raw 16-plex TMT assay that still includes the carrier channel.\\
\textit{Mathematical Consequence:} Because the carrier contains 100-fold more signal than single cells, global normalization algorithms align single-cell intensities to carrier fluctuations, distorting single-cell abundance measurements.\\
\textit{Remedy:} Exclude carrier and empty channels using \texttt{subsetByColData()} prior to calculating single-cell normalization factors.
\end{pitfallbox}

\begin{pitfallbox}
\textbf{Deadly Sin 5: Clustering Unnormalized Raw Reporter Intensities}\\
\textit{Mechanism:} Executing PCA, UMAP, or Leiden clustering directly on raw single-cell reporter ion intensities without sample loading adjustment.\\
\textit{Mathematical Consequence:} PC1 reflects total cell volume, pipetting volume, or channel labeling efficiency rather than biological cell identity. Cells cluster by acquisition run or cell diameter.\\
\textit{Remedy:} Normalize by total cell reporter sum or median, log2-transform, and center features prior to dimensionality reduction.
\end{pitfallbox}

\subsection*{Solution to Exercise 26.1: Poisson Sampling Limits}

\textbf{1. Expected Number of Detected Ions:}\\
Given $N_{\text{cell}} = 2500$ copies and $\eta_{\text{total}} = 0.40\% = 0.0040$:
\begin{equation}
\lambda = N_{\text{cell}} \cdot \eta_{\text{total}} = 2500 \times 0.0040 = 10 \;\text{ions}
\end{equation}

\textbf{2. Theoretical Minimum Poisson CV:}\\
From Eq.~(\ref{eq:poisson_cv}):
\begin{equation}
\text{CV}_{\text{Poisson}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{10}} \approx 0.3162 \;\text{(31.62\%)}
\end{equation}
Even under perfectly noiseless electronics, the physical quantum measurement uncertainty is 31.6\%.

\textbf{3. Impact of Carrier Channel Restriction:}\\
When total trap capacity is $C_{\text{cap}} = 200{,}000$ charges, and the single-cell channel receives only 2\% of capacity:
\begin{equation}
C_{\text{single}} = 0.02 \times 200{,}000 = 4{,}000 \;\text{charges total across all peptides}
\end{equation}
If this kinase peptide represents $0.1\%$ of the single-cell proteome:
\begin{equation}
\lambda' = 4{,}000 \times 0.001 = 4 \;\text{ions}
\end{equation}
The revised Poisson CV is:
\begin{equation}
\text{CV}' = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4}} = \frac{1}{2} = 0.5000 \;\text{(50.0\%)}
\end{equation}
Excessive carrier trapping degraded single-cell precision from 31.6\% to 50.0\%. \hfill $\blacksquare$

\subsection*{Solution to Exercise 26.2: SCoPE2 Carrier Ratio Compression}

Given: $R_{\text{true}} = I_1 / I_2 = 3.0 \implies I_1 = 3.0 \, I_2$. Carrier loading: $I_{\text{carrier}} = 100 \, I_2$. Contamination fraction: $f = 0.15$.

\textbf{1. Observed Reporter Intensities:}\\
\begin{align}
I_1^{\text{obs}} &= I_1 + 0.15 \, I_{\text{carrier}} = 3.0 \, I_2 + 0.15(100 \, I_2) = 3.0 \, I_2 + 15.0 \, I_2 = 18.0 \, I_2 \\
I_2^{\text{obs}} &= I_2 + 0.15 \, I_{\text{carrier}} = 1.0 \, I_2 + 0.15(100 \, I_2) = 1.0 \, I_2 + 15.0 \, I_2 = 16.0 \, I_2
\end{align}

\textbf{2. Observed Ratio and Compression:}\\
\begin{equation}
R_{\text{obs}} = \frac{I_1^{\text{obs}}}{I_2^{\text{obs}}} = \frac{18.0 \, I_2}{16.0 \, I_2} = \frac{18.0}{16.0} = 1.125
\end{equation}
The true fold change of $3.00$ ($\log_2 \text{FC} = 1.585$) was compressed to $1.125$ ($\log_2 \text{FC} = 0.170$).

\textbf{3. Percentage Signal Compression:}\\
\begin{equation}
\text{Compression} = \left( 1 - \frac{R_{\text{obs}} - 1}{R_{\text{true}} - 1} \right) \times 100\% = \left( 1 - \frac{1.125 - 1}{3.00 - 1} \right) \times 100\% = \left( 1 - \frac{0.125}{2.00} \right) \times 100\% = 93.75\%
\end{equation}
Over 93.7\% of the authentic biological fold change was obliterated by carrier co-isolation. \hfill $\blacksquare$

audit_scp_channels <- function(mat, blank_name, min_features = 250, max_doublet_ratio = 2.5) {
  # 1. Blank Channel Intensity Baseline
  blank_intensity <- median(mat[, blank_name], na.rm = TRUE)
  
  # 2. Per-Channel Metrics
  tot_intensity <- colSums(mat, na.rm = TRUE)
  med_intensity <- apply(mat, 2, median, na.rm = TRUE)
  n_detected    <- colSums(!is.na(mat) & mat > 0)
  
  # Compute ratio over blank
  signal_to_blank <- med_intensity / blank_intensity
  
  # Median single-cell intensity across non-blank channels
  sc_channels <- setdiff(colnames(mat), blank_name)
  median_sc   <- median(tot_intensity[sc_channels])
  doublet_ratio <- tot_intensity / median_sc
  
  # 3. Classify Status
  status <- rep("PASS", ncol(mat))
  status[colnames(mat) == blank_name] <- "CONTROL_BLANK"
  status[n_detected < min_features | signal_to_blank < 3.0] <- "FLAG_EMPTY_WELL"
  status[doublet_ratio > max_doublet_ratio & colnames(mat) != blank_name] <- "FLAG_DOUBLET"
  
  qc_summary <- data.frame(
    Channel       = colnames(mat),
    Features_Count= n_detected,
    Total_Signal  = tot_intensity,
    Signal_To_Blank= round(signal_to_blank, 2),
    Doublet_Ratio = round(doublet_ratio, 2),
    Status        = status,
    stringsAsFactors = FALSE
  )
  
  cat("--- Single-Cell Channel Quality Control Summary ---\n")
  print(qc_summary)
  return(invisible(qc_summary))
}

\subsection*{Solution to Exercise 26.4: Hierarchical Mixed Model vs. Naive t-Test}

\begin{rchunk}{Demonstration of Pseudoreplication and Type I Error Inflation}
# Simulate Null Dataset: No true condition difference between Condition A and B
# 60 Single Cells across 6 Donors (3 Donors per condition, 10 cells per donor)
set.seed(999)

n_donors <- 6
cells_per_donor <- 10
n_total_cells <- n_donors * cells_per_donor

donor_ids <- rep(paste0("Donor_", 1:n_donors), each = cells_per_donor)
conditions <- rep(c("Condition_A", "Condition_B"), each = n_total_cells / 2)

# Donor Random Intercept Effect (ICC = 0.40)
donor_random_effects <- rnorm(n_donors, mean = 0, sd = 1.0)
donor_effect_expanded <- rep(donor_random_effects, each = cells_per_donor)

# Repeat 1,000 simulations under the Null Hypothesis
p_naive_list <- c()
p_lmm_list   <- c()

suppressPackageStartupMessages(library(lme4))

for (sim in 1:200) {
  # Null response with donor clustering
  cell_noise <- rnorm(n_total_cells, mean = 0, sd = 1.2)
  y_null <- 10 + donor_effect_expanded + cell_noise
  
  # 1. Naive t-test (ignoring donor clustering)
  p_naive <- t.test(y_null ~ conditions)$p.value
  p_naive_list <- c(p_naive_list, p_naive)
  
  # 2. Linear Mixed Model (accounting for donor random intercept)
  df_sim <- data.frame(y = y_null, condition = conditions, donor = donor_ids)
  fit_lmm <- lmer(y ~ condition + (1 | donor), data = df_sim, REML = FALSE)
  fit_null <- lmer(y ~ 1 + (1 | donor), data = df_sim, REML = FALSE)
  p_lmm <- anova(fit_null, fit_lmm)[2, "Pr(>Chisq)"]
  p_lmm_list <- c(p_lmm_list, p_lmm)
}

false_pos_naive <- mean(p_naive_list < 0.05) * 100
false_pos_lmm   <- mean(p_lmm_list < 0.05) * 100

cat("--- Pseudoreplication Type I Error Audit (200 Null Simulations) ---\n")
cat(sprintf("Naive Two-Sample t-Test False Positive Rate: %.1f%% (Catastrophic Error Inflation!)\n",
            false_pos_naive))
cat(sprintf("Linear Mixed Model (LMM) False Positive Rate:   %.1f%% (Preserves alpha = 5%%)\n",
            false_pos_lmm))
\end{rchunk}
